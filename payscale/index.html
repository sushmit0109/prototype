The short answer

A doubling that does not cover the decade it is paying for

Between the 2015 scale and the 2026 scale, consumer prices in Bangladesh rose 115.3%. The scale rose 100%. That gap, and the way the shortfall is distributed between juniors and seniors, is the whole story.

Accounting for seniority

Steps collapse, and officers move down the ladder

Article 5 adds the officer's distance above the old starting step to the new starting step, then rounds up to the next printed step. Because the 2026 ladder climbs in smaller proportional jumps, officers who were on different steps land on the same one — and almost everybody lands lower in the ladder than they stood. Each line below is one step of the old scale finding its place in the new.

Reduction to a lower stage is normally a punishment

Rule 4(3) of the Government Servants (Discipline and Appeal) Rules 2018 lists বেতন স্কেলের নিম্ন ধাপে অবনমিতকরণ — reduction to a lower stage in the pay scale — as a major penalty, requiring a charge, a hearing and a right of appeal. Here the fixation formula moves most officers down the ladder as a matter of arithmetic, with no process and no finding against anyone.

Macro test

Is the jump justified?

Three benchmarks, all indexed to 2015 = 100: the consumer price index, which says whether pay holds its purchasing power; nominal GDP per head, which says whether pay holds its share of what the country produces; and real income per head at purchasing power parity, which says whether public servants share in growth. Purchasing power parity is the wrong instrument here

PPP exists to compare one country's pay with another's. A Bangladeshi civil servant buys rice, rent and school fees in taka, at home, so the test of whether a raise is adequate is the domestic consumer price index — and on that test the scale is short. The PPP series is shown because it answers a different and equally awkward question: real income per head grew — over the same period, which the pay scale did not follow.

Is each order fixing what the last one broke?

The same four pathologies recur at every transition: steps collapse, officers are demoted within the ladder, the raise falls as seniority rises, and the real value of the previous scale has already been eaten before the new one lands. Inter-service

One ladder for everyone, for exactly one pay scale

For decades the same grade number paid different money depending on which service an officer was in. The 2015 order ended that and put every cadre on one ladder. The 2026 order applies to the civil service alone.

How this was built, and what to distrust

The 17 September 2026 gazette prints the 2015 and 2026 ladders side by side, step by step, which makes it the authoritative source for both. Every step amount shown for those two scales was read from that table and then checked twice: each ladder must rise strictly, and each step must sit within a tight band of the ladder's own median step ratio. Fifteen OCR lines failed those checks — the recogniser renders some Bengali numerals as Latin digits, and dropped one line outright — and each was re-read from the page image and recorded as an explicit override. Scales from 1973 to 2009 are expanded from the increment bands the gazettes print (7x490;11x540), cross-checked against the same scale as reprinted in other service chapters.

Prices are a chained index: World Bank WDI consumer price index (FP.CPI.TOTL) from 1986, extended back to 1980 and forward through 2028 with IMF World Economic Outlook inflation. GDP per head is World Bank NY.GDP.PCAP.CN and NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD. Figures from the 1970s rest on the GDP deflator rather than a consumer price index.

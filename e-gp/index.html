Finding 01 — Timing

One in every seven contracts is signed in June — the last month of the fiscal year. In August, it's one in twenty-two.

Bangladesh's fiscal year ends 30 June. Contract signings cluster hard against that deadline and collapse immediately after it, the classic signature of year-end budget flush: money that must be committed before it lapses.

Why it matters

Procurement rushed to beat a deadline gets less scrutiny, fewer bidders, and weaker price discipline than the same purchase made in October. The spike is a standing indicator of where quality risk concentrates.

What it isn't

Not evidence of wrongdoing. Some seasonality is legitimate — construction avoids the monsoon, and annual supply contracts naturally renew on the fiscal calendar.

Finding 02 — Competition

In 2015, 94% of contract value went through fully open tendering. It collapsed to 66% in 2019 and has never returned to its old level.

Open Tendering (OTM) is the default competitive route: anyone qualified may bid. The value that left it went to limited, invitation-only tendering (LTM) and to two-envelope variants (OSTETM) — both narrower processes. The share has partly rebounded since, ranging from 70% to 80%, but it has not been near 90% in any year since 2017.

Why it matters

Openness is the single strongest structural defence against both overpricing and favouritism. A 20-point drop in the share of money exposed to open competition is a material change in how the state buys.

What it isn't

Limited tendering is legal and appropriate for small or urgent purchases. The chart shows a shift in mix, not a count of violations.

Finding 03 — Plan vs outcome

— of contracts are awarded at almost exactly 10% below the government's own estimate. Another — land on exactly 5% below.

The Annual Procurement Plan records, before any bidding happens, what each package should cost. Competitive bidding against that estimate would produce a smooth spread of outcomes. What the data shows instead is two narrow spikes on round discount rates — the signature of a discount applied by convention rather than discovered by competition.

And when the buying method changes, it changes one way

Most packages are bought exactly as planned. Among those that aren't, a package becomes less open than planned — times as often as it becomes more open — overwhelmingly open tendering turning into limited tendering.

Why it matters

A price that lands on a fixed discount off a published estimate is a price nobody competed for. And a method downgrade is the quietest way to narrow a competition: the package is still procured, still recorded, still legal-looking — only fewer firms ever got to bid. Neither is visible unless you hold the plan and the award side by side.

A conventional discount is not itself illegal, and some agencies may set ceilings this way deliberately. The finding is that the number is being set, not that a rule was broken.

Why it matters

A buyer that always picks the same seller has, in practice, stopped running a market — whatever the paperwork says. These are the specific places to audit first.

What it isn't

Concentration has innocent causes: sole regional suppliers, one multi-year project split into many contracts, or genuinely specialised goods. Each row needs its own look.

Finding 05 — Pricing

One in five contracts is priced at one of just twenty values — and those cluster far more heavily on invitation-only awards than contracts generally do.

Tk 9,50,000 alone appears — times, roughly eight times its neighbouring prices. Across the top twenty repeated prices, — of contracts were awarded by limited tendering, against a — baseline across all contracts. The single most repeated prices run as high as 93% limited-tender.

Why it matters

Identical prices recurring thousands of times across unrelated offices suggests a price being set to a convention rather than discovered by competition — and the strong tilt toward invitation-only awards is consistent with that.

What it isn't

The portal quotes value in crore to three decimals, so all values snap to Tk 10,000 steps. That rounding is uniform and cannot manufacture a spike — but it does mean each "price" is really a Tk 10,000-wide band.

Methodology

How this was built, and where it can be wrong

The false-positive that nearly shipped

Matching contract winners against the debarment register on company name alone produced 8,707 "active violation" flags worth ৳53.4 billion. It was wrong. The largest contributor, khan enterprise, accounted for 637 of them: "Khan" is among the most common surnames in Bangladesh and "Enterprise" a generic suffix. That name appears on 1,984 contracts across 15+ districts, while the register holds exactly one such firm at one Dhaka address — dozens of unrelated small businesses, not one company evading a ban.

Flags now require the contract's district to match the debarred firm's registered address, and exclude any name appearing as a winner in more than five districts nationwide. That cut 8,707 to 246. The filter deliberately trades missed real violations for fewer false accusations.

Derived vs. reported — what the source never actually said
  • Debarment severity is computed here (scope breadth × duration × repeat offences). The register publishes no rating.
  • Reason categories are keyword-tagged from free text written by whichever office filed the debarment.
  • "Mostly buys" joins procurement nature from the tender list, which matches only 53% of contracts — thinner before 2018.
  • Concentration is tested only on offices with 50+ contracts and 50+ crore in spend; below that, one big contract looks like a monopoly.

Ownership matching

Award detail pages carry a Tenderer ID and a beneficial-ownership table, but only on recent awards — a mid-2024 award page has neither field; an August 2026 one has both, tracking a 2025-era rules change. Rather than fetch all 871,684 contract pages for data that doesn't vary per contract, one representative (most recent) contract is sampled per vendor, largest vendors first.

Owner names are matched only when the normalised name has three or more parts, which reduces but does not remove collisions. Every group needs a human check that the companies form a real corporate family before it means anything.

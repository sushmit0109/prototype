Methodology & honest caveats

How the debarment-matching works, and why the first version was wrong

A name-only first pass -- match a contract's awardee against the debarment register by normalised company name, no other check -- produced 8,707 "active violation" flags worth ৳53.4 billion. It was wrong. The single biggest contributor, company_key "khan enterprise", accounted for 637 of them: "Khan" is one of the most common surnames in Bangladesh and "Enterprise" a generic small-firm suffix, and that exact name string appears on 1,984 contracts across 15+ districts nationwide, while the debarment register has exactly one such firm, registered at one address in Mirpur, Dhaka. That's obviously dozens of unrelated small businesses sharing a name pattern, not one company evading a ban -- and publishing that number would have been a real, public false accusation against real, named companies.

A flag now requires two things, not one: the contract's district must match a district taken from the debarment record's own address, and the company name must not appear, as an awardee, in more than 5 districts nationwide under otherwise-unrelated contracts (the signature of a common name pattern rather than one entity). That cut the result to 246 flags across 58 companies. This is still a heuristic, not proof, and it deliberately trades missed real violations for fewer false accusations -- a real per-company ID (which none of the portal's sources currently expose consistently across both the debarment register and the award list) would be needed to do better.

What's derived vs. what the source actually says
  • Debarment severity is not an official rating -- the source gives none. It combines scope breadth (Single Tender vs. nationwide e-GP Portal ban), duration, and repeat-offender count.
  • Reason tags on debarment records are a keyword-tagged guess (collusion / forgery / fraud / corruption / non-performance / misrepresentation), not an official category -- "Reasons" is free text written by whichever office filed it.
  • Vendor concentration flags single-buyer dependence, not wrongdoing -- specialised suppliers concentrate for entirely ordinary reasons.

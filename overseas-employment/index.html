Remittance per migrant

Money sent home divided by the number of Bangladeshis actually living in each country (UN DESA, 2024) — not by recent clearances. Clearances count new workers over three years; remittance comes from everyone settled abroad, including decades of earlier migration, so dividing money by clearances would compare a flow with a stock and say more about when a diaspora formed than about what it earns. Countries with fewer than 20,000 Bangladeshi residents are omitted — a small denominator swings the per-head figure wildly. The stock is a 2024 snapshot applied across the selected years, so earlier years are approximate.

Source: Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training (BMET) / Overseas Employment Portal, oep.gov.bd. District boundaries from geoBoundaries (BGD ADM2); world boundaries from Natural Earth via world-atlas. District population from the 2022 census via UN OCHA's Common Operational Dataset (UNFPA / BBS). District remittance from Bangladesh Bank's Monthly Report on Workers' Remittance Inflows (Annex-IV), million USD by fiscal year, FY2017-18 onward.

Reading this data. A clearance is permission to take up a job abroad — it counts intent to travel, not arrivals, and one person may appear once per clearance. Districts are the worker's home district, not their route. About 1.3% of records carry no district and are excluded from the maps. 2023-06-19 is excluded entirely: it is a catch-all bucket in the source that keeps absorbing records rather than a real day. Figures for the most recent days are incomplete while data entry continues.
Remittance. A different quantity, not another cut of the same one. Bangladesh Bank credits remittance to the recipient's district, while a clearance records the worker's home district. Dhaka receives 34.8% of all remittance but accounts for 4.1% of clearances — that is where bank accounts and head offices are, not where migrants come from. For the same reason this dashboard does not show “USD per clearance”: it would put Dhaka at 15.6× the national median and read as a migration finding when it is a banking artefact. Remittance is annual (FY2017-18 onward) and has no destination breakdown, so the country filter does not apply to it.
Per 100,000. The rate divides clearances in the selected period by each district's 2022 census population — how many residents out of every 100,000 took a clearance, not how many people live there. Per 100k · 15–64 uses only working-age residents (67.0% of the population nationally), which is the closer denominator since almost every migrant is of working age. Both are propensity measures and both are more sensitive in small districts. Population is fixed at the 2022 census, so internal migration since then is not reflected.
